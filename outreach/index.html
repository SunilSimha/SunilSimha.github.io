Outreach

My research without the jargon, in talks and short explainers.

I study fast radio bursts: flashes of radio waves from distant galaxies that last about a thousandth of a second. This page explains what they are and what we learn from them, for readers with no astronomy background.

Watch

Uncovering the hidden universe

An eight-minute talk for a general audience, recorded at Northwestern University: how background light sources such as fast radio bursts help us reveal invisible gas in space. This was my final presentation for the excellent RCTP course.

Astronomy on Tap: Fast Radio Bursts

A public talk with Jay Chittidi at Astronomy on Tap Santa Cruz (November 2020): how fast radio bursts were discovered, what we know about them, and how they let us measure where the universe keeps its matter.

FRBs as probes of foreground matter

A lecture for student researchers at the Fast Radio Transient Sky (FTSky) programme, ICTS Bengaluru (October 2025): an overview of optical follow-up of fast radio bursts and what the bursts tell us about the gas in front of them.
